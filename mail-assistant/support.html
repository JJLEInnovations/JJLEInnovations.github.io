Mail Assistant Support

Email jjleinnovations@gmail.com and you will get a reply from a person. Below are the questions that come up most.

Getting started

The app is asking for access to a folder. Why?

Mail Assistant reads the mail Apple Mail has already downloaded, which lives in your ~/Library/Mail folder. macOS requires you to point the app at that folder yourself, in a standard file panel, before it can read anything. You only do this once.

It does not need Full Disk Access, and granting this does not give the app access to anything else on your Mac.

It is asking for permission to control Mail.

That prompt appears the first time you confirm an action. Moving a message or marking it read has to be done by Apple Mail itself, and macOS requires your approval before one app can ask another to do something. Searching does not need it — only changes do.

Everything is empty.

Check Settings › Apple Mail access first. If it says Not granted, the app cannot read anything until you choose Apple Mail’s folder — that is the usual cause, and it is also what happens if you used Revoke Access.

If access is granted, the app is reading an Apple Mail setup that has nothing in it yet. If Mail is not signed in to an account, or has not finished downloading messages, there is nothing to show. Open Mail, make sure your mail is there, then use Refresh Report.

Using it

I moved Gmail messages and they are still in the Inbox.

This is Gmail, not a bug, and the app warns you before it happens.

Gmail does not have folders. It has labels, and a message can carry several at once. When an app asks Apple Mail to file a Gmail message, Gmail adds the new label but keeps the old one, so the message appears in both places. There is no command Apple Mail can be asked to run that removes a Gmail label. That is a limit of Apple Mail’s scripting rather than of Gmail — Gmail’s own web interface removes labels perfectly well, and so does its API, but reaching that API would mean sending your mail off this Mac. It affects every app that works through Apple Mail.

To end up in one place only, move the messages in Apple Mail itself, which files them properly, or remove the old label in Gmail on the web. Moving to Trash does work normally on Gmail, because trashing removes every label.

Can I undo something?

Yes. Restore puts back messages you moved to Trash, returning each one to the folder it came from. Mark as Read is undone by Mark as Unread, which appears on the same button once a sender has nothing unread left.

Every action is recorded in Recent actions, so you can see exactly what happened and when.

Why does a large cleanup take a while?

Because Apple Mail makes every change, not this app. Mail Assistant never writes to your mail files. It asks Apple Mail to move or mark each message, one at a time, so your accounts stay in sync and nothing happens to your mail that Apple Mail did not do itself.

Where Mail Assistant stands out is the work around that: seeing which senders are worth acting on across all your accounts at once, then handling all of them in a single confirmed pass instead of searching for each one by hand. For one sender already in front of you, Apple Mail is quicker. For two hundred you have not looked at in a year, this is one pass rather than two hundred searches — and the window stays usable, with a Stop button, for as long as it runs.

Is it safe to use the unsubscribe button?

It depends on the sender, and the app tells you which case you are in.

Mail Assistant never unsubscribes you. It reads the unsubscribe details the sender put in their own message headers — not a link buried in the artwork, which is the one security guidance tells you to avoid — and hands you the route they published. A web address opens in your browser; an email address opens a pre-addressed message in Apple Mail that is not sent until you send it.

Either route tells the sender your address is live, because the link they publish identifies you. For a shop or a newsletter you signed up for, that is fine and unsubscribing is the right move. For a sender you have never heard of, it is the wrong tool: it confirms a real person reads the mailbox, and some unsubscribe pages exist to find that out. Mark one of their messages as Junk in Apple Mail instead, and use Move to Trash here for what already arrived.

Because the app can see whether you have ever written to an address, it says which of those two you are looking at before you choose.

What is the Trends tab for?

Deciding what to deal with, rather than doing it. It shows how much mail arrived each week and when the busiest week was, senders who first wrote recently and whose mail you have never once opened, addresses you have not heard from in over a year, and what is stacking up unread now. The sender lists can be narrowed to addresses you have never written to, so people you correspond with stay out of the way.

Nothing on that tab changes your mail. Each row has a Show button that opens the sender in the Senders Report, where you can act on it.

Is my mail sent anywhere?

No. The app has no network permission at all, so macOS will not let it connect to the internet even if it tried. See the privacy policy for the detail.

Removing the app

The same four steps are in the app, under Settings › Removing Mail Assistant, with a button that opens the folder for you.

  1. If you switched on Launch at Login, choose Disable Launch at Login from the app’s envelope icon in the menu bar, then quit it from there.
  2. Delete the app the way you delete any other — from Launchpad, or by dragging it from Applications to the Trash.
  3. Drag its folder to the Trash: ~/Library/Containers/com.jjleinnovations.MailAssistant/. The Finder shows this folder as Mail Assistant. That takes the search index and the action history with it.
  4. Optional: open System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation and remove Mail Assistant. macOS keeps that entry after an app is deleted.

Your mail is untouched by any of this. The app never stored a copy of it, and anything you moved or deleted with it stays as it is in Apple Mail.
