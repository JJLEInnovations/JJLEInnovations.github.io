Mail Assistant

An assistant for Apple Mail — it works alongside Mail, on the mail already on your Mac, and shows you what Mail cannot.

Everything stays on this Mac. Mail Assistant has no network permission, no account, and no analytics. It reads the mail Apple Mail already downloaded and never sends any of it anywhere.

What makes it stand out

Apple Mail handles your mail well. What it does not do is show you the shape of it. It can tell you how big an account or a mailbox is, but not which senders are behind that size, how many messages each has sent, how much space they take, or which of them you have never once opened.

Mail Assistant answers those questions, then lets you act on the answer — across every account at once, with a confirmation first and a way back afterwards. Visibility, and the tools to do something with it.

What it does

See who is actually filling your inbox

The Senders Report ranks every sender by how many messages they have sent you and how much space those take — and sorting by size is usually the surprise. A handful of senders account for most of a mailbox. Each one opens out to show which folders their mail is sitting in.

Catch it before it piles up

Trends watches the shape of your mail rather than its contents: how much arrived each week and when the busiest week was, new senders you have never once opened, addresses you have not heard from in over a year, and what is stacking up unread now. Those lists can be narrowed to addresses you have never written to, so the people you actually correspond with stay out of the way.

Trends changes nothing by itself. It tells you where to look.

Search once, then act on what you find

By sender, subject, date, folder, attachment name, or words inside the message body — all of it from an index kept on your Mac. The results are not a dead end: each row is tagged with the account it came from, and you can select any of them and act on the whole selection at once. Searches you run often can be saved, and any result list can be exported as a spreadsheet.

Act on hundreds of messages in one pass

Move to a folder, move to Trash, or mark read or unread — for several senders at once, across different accounts, in a single run. Every action shows a confirmation naming exactly how many messages it will affect, reports back how many were actually changed, and can be stopped part way without losing the work already done.

Stop the next one from arriving

Deleting today’s newsletter does nothing about next week’s. Mail Assistant reads the unsubscribe details the sender already put in their own message headers and hands the link to your browser. It also tells you whether unsubscribing is the right move: for a sender you have written to before it usually is, and for one you have never heard from it usually is not.

Put it back

Restore returns messages from Trash to the folders they came from. Mark as Read is undone by Mark as Unread. Recent actions keeps a record of everything you did, so nothing you try here is a one-way door.

You can see exactly what it is doing

Settings shows the folder access you granted and lets you withdraw it, how large the search index is and what it holds, and every mark you have put on a sender. Nothing about how the app works is hidden behind a preference you cannot inspect.

Requirements

macOS 14 or later, and Apple Mail signed in to at least one account. Mail Assistant works alongside Apple Mail rather than replacing it — your mail stays exactly where it is, in Apple Mail, and anything this app changes is a change Apple Mail itself carries out.
